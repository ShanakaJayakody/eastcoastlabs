Semax and selank are the two peptides most often studied together in neuro-signalling and behavioural research. This overview covers what distinguishes them, from a research perspective.

## The two compounds

Both are short synthetic peptides derived from naturally occurring regulatory peptides, developed originally in Russian research programmes and now widely used as reference compounds in neuroscience research.

- **[Semax](/product/semax)** — a synthetic peptide related to a fragment of **adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH)**, studied in neuro-signalling and neurotrophic research.
- **[Selank](/product/selank)** — a synthetic analogue of the immunomodulatory peptide **tuftsin**, studied in anxiolytic-behaviour and neuroimmune research models.

Each is supplied lyophilised with an independently published [Certificate of Analysis](/lab-results) per batch. Together they form our [Cognitive & Focus stack](/stacks).

## Areas of scientific research interest

Published literature has explored these peptides in experimental models related to:

- Neuro-signalling and neurotrophic-factor research
- Behavioural and cognitive research models
- Neuroimmune and stress-response signalling studies
- Peptide stability and blood-brain-barrier research

See also our [Cognitive & Focus collection](/collections/cognitive-focus).

> These describe *research contexts* only, not efficacy claims. Neither compound is approved for therapeutic use, and neither is supplied for human or animal consumption.
