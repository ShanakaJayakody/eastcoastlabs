Research peptides are supplied as a **lyophilised** (freeze-dried) powder for stability during storage and transport. Before they can be used in solution, they must be reconstituted — dissolved in a suitable sterile diluent. Done carefully, reconstitution is simple; done carelessly, it can degrade the peptide or introduce contamination that ruins an experiment.

## What you need

- The lyophilised peptide vial, brought to **room temperature** (cold glass encourages condensation)
- **Bacteriostatic water** — sterile water containing a small amount of benzyl alcohol as a preservative, which allows the reconstituted solution to be stored for longer than sterile water alone
- A sterile syringe for measuring and transferring diluent
- Alcohol prep swabs and a clean, aseptic working surface

You can add [bacteriostatic water and syringes](/shop) to any order.

## Step by step

1. **Equalise temperature.** Let both the vial and the diluent reach room temperature before you begin.
2. **Swab the stoppers.** Wipe the rubber stopper of both the peptide vial and the water vial with an alcohol swab.
3. **Add the water slowly.** Draw your chosen volume of bacteriostatic water and inject it **down the inside wall of the vial**, not directly onto the powder. A gentle stream protects the delicate peptide structure.
4. **Do not shake.** Swirl the vial gently, or simply leave it to stand. The powder will dissolve on its own within a minute or two. Shaking introduces shear forces and foaming that can damage peptides.
5. **Inspect.** A properly reconstituted solution should be **clear and free of particulates**. Cloudiness or floating material is a sign of a problem.
6. **Record the concentration.** Note how much peptide and how much water you used, so you know the resulting concentration for your records.

## Working out the concentration

Concentration is simply the mass of peptide divided by the volume of diluent. For example, a 10&nbsp;mg vial reconstituted with 2&nbsp;ml of water gives a solution of 5&nbsp;mg/ml.

Our [reconstitution concentration calculator](/learn) does this maths for you and expresses the result per insulin unit (1 unit = 0.01&nbsp;ml) for convenient record-keeping. It is a concentration reference only — not dosing guidance.

## Storage after reconstitution

Once in solution, keep the vial **refrigerated and sealed** between uses, and use aseptic technique each time you draw from it. The benzyl alcohol in bacteriostatic water suppresses microbial growth, but it is not a substitute for careful handling.
