The incretin analogues — semaglutide, tirzepatide and retatrutide — are among the most-studied peptides in metabolic research. This overview explains what distinguishes them, from a research standpoint.

## The incretin class

**GLP-1 (glucagon-like peptide-1)** and **GIP (glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide)** are incretin hormones involved in glucose and energy-regulation signalling. Synthetic analogues of these hormones are widely used as reference compounds in metabolic and endocrine research models.

The three compounds differ by how many incretin receptors they engage:

- **[Semaglutide](/product/semaglutide)** — a GLP-1 receptor analogue (single-agonist)
- **[Tirzepatide](/product/tirzepatide)** — a dual GLP-1 / GIP receptor analogue
- **[Retatrutide](/product/retatrutide)** — a triple GLP-1 / GIP / glucagon receptor analogue

Each is supplied lyophilised with an independently published [Certificate of Analysis](/lab-results) per batch.

## Areas of scientific research interest

Preclinical and analytical literature examines this class in experimental models related to:

- Incretin-receptor signalling and binding studies
- Glucose- and energy-metabolism research models
- Neuroendocrine and appetite-signalling pathway research
- Comparative receptor-agonism studies (single vs dual vs triple)

For research comparing metabolic pathways, tirzepatide and the mitochondrial-derived peptide [MOTS-C](/product/mots-c) are grouped in our [Metabolic Research stack](/stacks).
