A Certificate of Analysis (COA) is the single most important document in research-peptide supply. It is the lab's written record of what a specific batch actually contains — and it is the only thing that separates a verifiable supplier from a marketing claim. If a supplier cannot show you a batch-specific COA from an independent laboratory, you have no way of knowing what is in the vial.

## What a COA measures

A complete peptide COA reports several distinct things. Each answers a different question:

- **Identity** — is this actually the peptide it claims to be? Confirmed by mass spectrometry (the measured molecular weight should match the theoretical weight of the sequence).
- **Purity** — what percentage of the material is the target peptide, versus related impurities, truncated sequences, or residual synthesis reagents? Measured by HPLC (high-performance liquid chromatography) and reported as a percentage of total peak area.
- **Batch / lot number** — ties the certificate to one specific production run. A COA with no batch number is effectively meaningless.
- **Test date and laboratory** — when the analysis was performed and by whom. An independent third-party lab is far stronger evidence than an in-house result.

## How to read the purity figure

Purity is reported as area-under-the-curve from the HPLC trace. A result of "≥ 99%" means the target peptide accounts for 99% or more of the detected material. For research applications, higher purity means fewer confounding variables — impurities can interfere with experimental results in ways that are difficult to control for.

Be wary of a purity claim printed only on a product page with no accompanying chromatogram. The chromatogram — the actual graph — is where purity is proven. A number on its own can be typed by anyone.

## Verifying a COA is genuine

The strongest verification is a **batch number you can check against the laboratory's own records**. This is why we publish every batch's COA before the product is listed and let you look up any vial by its batch number on our [Lab Results](/lab-results) page. The batch on your vial should match the batch on the published certificate, and the laboratory named should be reachable and independent.

Red flags:

- No batch number, or the same COA reused across every product
- Purity claimed but no chromatogram shown
- An "independent" lab that cannot be found or contacted
- A test date years old, or missing entirely

## Why this matters more in peptides than almost anywhere else

Research peptides are difficult to evaluate by eye — a vial of lyophilised powder tells you nothing about what it contains. The category has a long history of mislabelled and under-strength product. A published, batch-specific, independent COA is the mechanism that makes quality *checkable* rather than merely *claimed*. It is the reason we test every batch through an independent laboratory and publish the result before anything ships.
