GHK-Cu is a naturally occurring copper-binding tripeptide and one of the most-studied peptides in skin and extracellular-matrix research. This overview summarises what it is, from a research perspective.

## What GHK-Cu is

GHK-Cu is the tripeptide **glycyl-L-histidyl-L-lysine** bound to a **copper(II) ion**. The GHK sequence occurs naturally in human plasma, where its concentration declines with age. Its strong affinity for copper gives the reconstituted solution a characteristic **blue tint** — a useful visual identity cue.

At East Coast Labs, [GHK-Cu](/product/ghk-cu) is supplied lyophilised with an independently published [Certificate of Analysis](/lab-results) per batch.

## Areas of scientific research interest

Published literature has explored GHK and GHK-Cu in experimental models related to:

- Dermal and skin-research models
- Extracellular-matrix (collagen, elastin) remodelling studies
- Copper-transport and metalloprotein research
- Gene-expression and cellular-signalling studies

## Physical & handling properties

- **Format:** lyophilised powder (reconstitutes to a blue solution)
- **Class:** copper-binding tripeptide
- **Reconstitution:** dissolve gently in [bacteriostatic water](/learn/reconstituting-research-peptides); the blue colour is expected
- **Storage:** refrigerate and protect from light ([storage guide](/learn/storing-research-peptides))
