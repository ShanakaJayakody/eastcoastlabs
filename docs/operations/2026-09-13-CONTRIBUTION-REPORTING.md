# Contribution and customer reporting

This change ships code and an additive migration only. No live financial data, payment-provider settings, messages, or production migrations were changed during implementation.

Apply `storefront/supabase/migrations/20260913110000_order_economics.sql` through the normal reviewed release process before serving the updated admin. It adds the privileged `admin_order_item_economics` view, an RLS-protected actual variable-cost table, and an authorized, audited, revision-checked write RPC. No historical costs, tax rates, payment dates, or return values are backfilled.

## Operator workflow

1. Open **Admin → Reports → Contribution** for the desired paid-date period. Net order revenue includes shipping and deducts recorded refunds. Merchandise gross profit excludes shipping revenue and deducts allocated discounts and consumed product/gift COGS.
2. Open an incomplete order from that report. Expand **Actual variable order costs**. Enter actual AUD amounts for carrier, packaging/pick-pack, payment fees, additional unrecovered replacement costs, variable service, and acquisition. Enter verified zero explicitly; leave unknowns blank. Record source references in the notes. Saving preserves an audit entry and rejects a stale editor.
3. Keep categories mutually exclusive. Product and gift costs already belong in frozen COGS. Refund amounts already reduce revenue; a later refund transfer settlement is evidence of payment, not another expense. Only additional unrecovered replacement expenses belong in replacement costs. Put creator commissions in acquisition once. Do not count total campaign spend again if it has already been allocated to orders.
4. Enter the accountant-reconciled **tax adjustment** separately from expenses. This is the signed correction needed to convert recorded net receipts, frozen COGS and the five operating expense categories to a consistent net-of-applicable-tax basis. A positive adjustment reduces contribution; a negative adjustment represents a reconciled credit and increases it. Exclude acquisition from this adjustment: enter acquisition expense on the resulting basis. The application does not calculate or assume GST rates, recoverable credits or registration. Confirm the reconciliation with the accountant before checking the basis confirmation. Record source references and update the adjustment if a refund or cost correction changes it. Blank remains unknown; an explicit verified zero is valid. Allowed range is −2,147,483,648 to 2,147,483,647 integer cents (−21,474,836.48 to 21,474,836.47 AUD).

   Before-acquisition contribution = recorded net receipts − consumed frozen COGS − five actual operating expense categories − signed tax adjustment. For example, $110 receipts less $55 total gross COGS/operating costs less a $5 reconciled net GST adjustment gives $50 before acquisition. This example illustrates entered figures, not an inferred tax rate.
5. Contribution before acquisition requires complete COGS and all five operating expense categories, an explicit tax adjustment (including verified zero), and a confirmed reconciled tax basis. Contribution after acquisition additionally requires actual acquisition expense. A missing required value leaves the aggregate unknown; coverage shows complete orders over all paid orders.

## Refunds and historical limitations

The original September 8 commerce migration backfilled `returned_qty` using historical refund counters in some cases. A refund alone is not evidence of sellable returned stock. The reporting view therefore recognizes only unique committed reviewed refunds whose stored quote explicitly chose restocking. The recovered quantity is bounded by both the current line quantity and its returned counter. Retry commits cannot recover cost twice.

A no-restock refund deducts merchandise revenue while retaining consumed COGS. Refunded gifts retain COGS unless there is confirmed physical restock evidence. A fully refunded historical order contributes zero merchandise revenue even if its line refund amount was never updated. Historic returns without reviewed commit evidence remain consumed conservatively; reconcile them separately from physical/source records rather than trusting inferred counters. Changing today's product cost does not repair a missing historical cost snapshot.

Reports use actual `paid_at`, retaining later-refunded orders. Dashboard revenue-today uses Sydney midnight; seven- and thirty-day revenue use rolling elapsed windows up to now. They net recorded refunds and treat a fully refunded historical order as zero net revenue. Current fulfilment and pending-payment counts remain operational status counts. Revenue and queue query errors propagate rather than returning a false zero. Orders with paid statuses and no timestamp are excluded from paid-date reports and counted as a historical gap in Contribution. They need source payment evidence, not the creation date substituted as payment. Refunds/cost corrections update the original payment cohort as known now. These reports are not a refund-date cash ledger, closed-period accounts, historical as-of valuation, or net profit after overhead.

## Retention and maturity

The created-to-paid readout uses orders created in the selected period and a fixed seven-day operational horizon. Only orders at least seven days old enter the denominator; the numerator paid within those seven days. Payments later refunded still count as payments. The horizon is a reporting choice, not a verified transfer SLA or a website session conversion rate.

Cohorts use the first actual paid month in Australia/Sydney and normalized checkout email. Each customer's own 60/90 days must mature before entering that horizon's denominator. The second-purchase rate includes a second paid order within that horizon. Revenue and contribution sum orders paid through that horizon, including the first purchase, net of refunds and actual expenses known now. Unknown contribution on any included order keeps the cohort's contribution unknown; coverage is reported explicitly. Aliases and email changes may split a person. This is observed cumulative value, not predicted lifetime value or causal campaign lift.

Session conversion, contribution per visitor and holdout incrementality still require verified eligible-session counts and experiment joins. Ad spend allocation, actual invoices/fees, supplier cost snapshots, tax treatment and elapsed 60/90-day follow-up are business/data dependencies. The code invents none of them.

## Verification evidence

Hand-calculated regression first failed on existing code: 15,000 line revenue minus 1,500 allocated discount minus 9,500 COGS must produce 4,000 gross profit (29.6%), not 5,500 (36.7%). Missing costs formerly produced a 100% margin; no-restock refunds formerly recovered consumed costs; query errors formerly returned a successful zero/partial result. Those regressions pass after the shared economics change.

Tests cover missing/gift COGS, paid-date product/dashboard reconciliation, pagination beyond 500 rows, explicit error and 50,000-row-cap failures, mature seven-day payment conversion, 60/90-day second purchasing and values, unknown contribution coverage, authenticated expense actions, visible cost input validation, stale saves, SQL public-role denial, and explicit-zero versus null persistence. The isolated PGlite commerce integration creates a synthetic discounted order, pays it, records both return choices, replays commits and settles the refund; COGS and revenue reconcile without double subtraction. No production database was contacted.

Final review regressions reproduced the omitted tax adjustment ($55 incorrectly reported instead of $50) and creation-date dashboard revenue (orders created eight days earlier but paid today missing from today/seven-day cards). The revised tests cover signed correction bounds and audit persistence, explicit unknown/zero, the editor, paid-date pagination, refunded population treatment and visible query failures.
